## Carrots · slender Imperator type

Provisional asset ID `provisional-carrots-thumbnail-v2`.

Jared’s revision calls for a narrower leaf end and a less conical silhouette. Imperator is a long, slender, gradually tapering market carrot. Keep both orange roots whole. [Oregon State University Extension, Imperator shape](https://horticulture.oregonstate.edu/oregon-vegetables/carrots-eastern-oregon-0), [University of Georgia Cooperative Extension, root length and shoulder diameter](https://extension.uga.edu/publications/detail.html?number=B1175), [NC State Extension, carrot color and foliage](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/daucus-carota-subsp-sativus/common-name/carrots/).

**Prompt**

Create one isolated produce thumbnail for Orchard’s illustrated produce journal, as a naturalistic botanical watercolor with luminous layered washes, selective fine detail, subtle granulation and soft diffuse upper-left light. Use the Orchard illustration-language sheet as the style reference. Center the compact silhouette with ample transparent margins, at an apparent scale comparable to the staged root vegetable thumbnails. Keep the edible roots dominant and readable at 72 px. Deliver a square PNG at least 1024 px per side on genuine transparent alpha. Omit text, paper, tabletop, border, cast shadow, heavy outlines and glossy rendering.

Revise Orchard’s prior painting of two whole carrots. Change the root geometry of BOTH carrots to slender, mature Imperator market carrots. Make each orange root at least six times as long as its maximum shoulder width, measured perpendicular to its axis. Give each a small rounded shoulder at its crown, then a long narrow shaft with nearly parallel sides over the upper third and a slow taper along the middle. The thin lower third narrows gently to its pointed tip. No bulbous shoulder, broad triangular wedge, or straight steep cone from crown to point. Keep the close overlapping diagonal pair and natural warm orange of the earlier painting, with both narrow shoulders and both pointed tips visible. The leading carrot keeps a small tuft of feathery, finely divided olive-green carrot foliage attached at the crown. The second has short clipped stems. Reduce heavy horizontal score marks to a few fine surface marks. Show whole roots only.

**Check status** · Passed `checkMaster` as a 1420 × 1420 RGBA PNG. Against v1, both orange roots have distinctly narrower shoulders and a more gradual taper at 72 px. Both pointed tips remain visible. The green tuft covers more area than prompted. The roots remain the focal point. No cut section.

## Parsnips · cream-colored market type

Provisional asset ID `provisional-parsnips-thumbnail-v1`.

Pale cream taproots with full shoulders and a long taper, a small trimmed crown, and sparse root hairs. Whole specimens show the distinguishing shape. [UC Agriculture and Natural Resources, whole parsnip photograph](https://ucanr.edu/site/uc-master-gardeners-santa-clara-county/parsnip), [Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, root color and form](https://aggie-horticulture.tamu.edu/vegetable/guides/specialty-vegetables/parsnip/), [UF/IFAS, parsnip root morphology](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/mastergardener/resources/plantid/vegetables/parsnip/).

**Prompt**

Create one isolated produce thumbnail for Orchard’s illustrated produce journal, as a naturalistic botanical watercolor with luminous layered washes, selective fine detail, subtle granulation and soft diffuse upper-left light. Use the Orchard illustration-language sheet as the style reference. Center the compact silhouette with ample transparent margins, at an apparent scale comparable to the staged root vegetable thumbnails. Keep the edible roots dominant and readable at 72 px. Deliver a square PNG at least 1024 px per side on genuine transparent alpha. Omit text, paper, tabletop, border, cast shadow, heavy outlines and glossy rendering.

Paint a compact, partly overlapping pair of whole raw parsnips, a long leading root and a slightly smaller root following the same diagonal. Each cream-colored taproot starts at a low trimmed crown set into broad, gently rounded shoulders, tapers through the upper half, and continues into a long narrow tail. Use a few fine tan lateral root traces, a muted tan collar around the trimmed crown, and parchment-cream and light gray-brown within the painted root for volume. Keep the edges visible against pale ivory. Convey pale cream parsnip skin with natural warm and cool shadows. Trim off foliage and show whole roots only. Avoid orange roots, a purple crown, or a flat playing-card triangle.
